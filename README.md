# Bryan’s Arcade

The homepage lists six games with illustrated scenes. Ghost Pop, Dad Dug, and Eviction are playable; Pac-Bryan, Quarters Please, and Power Struggle are marked Coming soon. Filter the grid by availability. All artwork is drawn locally; no build or downloaded assets are required.

Game links use extensionless routes (`ghost-pop`, `dad-dug`, and `eviction`). Home links use `./`, resolving to the GitHub Pages project root, `https://mystictiger2.github.io/Pac-Bryan/`. Serve game routes as the matching `.html` files with `Content-Type: text/html`. Keep `arcade.js` and `arcade.css` alongside the pages, including when opening a game directly from disk.

## Quarters

The homepage and paid arcade games display a shared browser-local balance, starting at 5 quarters. Each new Ghost Pop game (including Custom), Dad Dug game, or Eviction game costs one quarter and plays a short coin-slot animation. Restart and Play again begin paid games; resuming, respawning, and advancing rounds or waves do not spend quarters. Eviction testing codes spend a quarter when launching from the menu or game-over screen, but modifying a current run is free. Duplicate starts are ignored during the coin animation. Web Locks serialize balance updates across tabs when available.

Balances persist under `tigerArcade.quarters.v1` in localStorage. Existing saved balances are preserved; a missing key starts at 5. Reloading or returning to the homepage does not reset an empty balance. Malformed balances are treated as zero. Unavailable localStorage falls back to sessionStorage, then page-local memory. With no storage at all, navigation/reloading cannot retain that page-local balance. Credits are local game data, not real currency or an account balance.

At zero, new paid games are blocked by an Out of quarters dialog with a “get more quarters” link directly to `quarters-please.html`. The homepage card also links to that file for now. Quarters Please is Arcade / Meta / Clicker and never charges admission or starts new runs. Its current page contains only a Home link and a red reset button. Reset opens a custom confirmation dialog; Cancel or Escape makes no changes, and confirming sets quarters to `min(5, current balance)` under the same cross-tab lock used for spending. Reset cannot add quarters. The clicker itself and its progress reset are not implemented yet, so the homepage retains Coming soon. Power Struggle is categorized as Idler / Tower Defense and does not use arcade credits.

## Eviction

Open `eviction.html`, or choose Eviction from the arcade. A modern Pac-Xon-style territory game: move off the green border, draw a line through the open board, and reconnect to your territory. Empty regions without a ghost become yours. The capture target starts at 80% of the interior on wave 1, rises by 1 percentage point per wave, and stays at 95% from wave 16 onward. The starting border does not count toward the target. Ghosts protect every empty cell their collision circle touches, including the empty half of orange boundary ghosts. Blue ghosts occupy green and do not block captures. If ghosts protect both sides of a cut, both sides remain open and only the line is claimed.

Move with arrows or WASD, hold the on-screen D-pad, or drag on the board to steer. Player movement is 5.5 cells per second, half the original speed. While drawing in empty space, release the controls and the player keeps moving in the last direction; steer to turn. On green, release to stop at the next cell center. Quick turns buffer until the next center; movement and enemy collisions use a fixed simulation step independently of the display refresh rate. Reverse along your unfinished line to undo it. Crossing another part of your own line still costs a life. Direct contact with orange, blue, or green ghosts can kill you on green; the other types catch you in the open. A lost life clears the unfinished line and all surges, then returns you to the last safe position while keeping surviving territory and score. If that position has been eroded, respawn moves to nearby green. Respawning grants 1.5 seconds of body-contact protection, shown by a dashed ring; surges remain dangerous.

When any ghost touches the actual drawn line, it ignites a red surge at the contact point instead of immediately taking a life. Detection includes the green anchor and moving tip, so blue ghosts can attack the start of a line before the first empty cell is completed. After a 0.12-second ignition warning, red starts at 8 cells per second and gains another 8 cells per second every active second: speeds of 8, 16, 24, 32, and so on, not exponential doubling. At active age `t`, speed is `8 × (1 + t)` and distance traveled is `8 × (t + t²/2)`. Surges follow bends in both directions along the line; each contact keeps its own acceleration clock even when red portions overlap. A surge costs a life if it reaches the player before they reconnect to green. Completing a line or retreating all the way to green extinguishes its surges. Closing over a touching ghost keeps its occupied cut cells open so it cannot be buried inside green. New games and waves reset all surges.

Each wave adds exactly one ghost. The first six introduce the six types in order; later waves cycle through the same roster, keeping earlier ghosts. Wave 10 has ten ghosts: two pink, two orange, two red, two blue, one purple, and one green.

| First wave | Ghost | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pink | Casual roaming and bouncing through empty space. Cannot damage green. |
| 2 | Orange | Rides the boundary between green and empty, including concave corners and new capture edges. Protects the regions its body overlaps. |
| 3 | Red | Bounces around empty space like a brickbreaker ball. Each hit chips one claimed interior tile and reflects it, with a short hit cooldown. |
| 4 | Blue/cyan | Starts on green from the beginning of the wave, replacing the old 10% unlock. Patrols on 95% of cell decisions and has only a 5% chance to steer toward the player. Patrols favor continuing their direction with occasional random turns and avoid needless reversals. It never crosses empty space. |
| 5 | Purple | Pauses for a 0.75-second “!” warning and telegraphs its aim, then charges toward that player position at 28.5 cells per second for up to 0.65 seconds. Green walls stop the charge. It recovers for 4.5–7 seconds before warning again. |
| 6 | Green | Plows through claimed interior blocks without bouncing or slowing. Changes direction naturally every 2.2–5 seconds and reflects only at the unbreakable outer border. |

All ghost movement is now 5% slower than the previous tuning, including red and purple's charge. Player speed and red-surge acceleration are unchanged. Terrain destruction immediately updates the claimed percentage, progress bar, orange routes, and blue paths. Blue moves to nearby surviving green if its footing or destination is destroyed. Orange keeps its contour when possible, or attaches to the nearest surviving boundary with brief body-contact grace. Grace does not stop line ignition. Separate empty regions have separate contour loops, including at diagonal meetings. Earned points are retained after erosion; the original border is unbreakable.

Space, X, or the on-screen Freeze button freezes all six ghost types for 2.5 seconds, stopping movement, destruction, purple warnings/charges, and green direction timers. Existing surges keep accelerating during Freeze; pause stops the entire simulation. Start each wave with zero charges. Reaching 45% and 90% claimed earns one charge each. Reclaiming lost territory does not repeatedly award the same milestone. Frozen ghosts still cause body-contact damage or ignite your line when touched. Touch supports steering and Freeze together. Standard gamepads use the left stick or D-pad to move, the south face button or right trigger to Freeze, and Start/Options to pause. South also starts, resumes, and advances waves. Release sticks and buttons after changing screens or returning to the page.

The sidebar uses two small before/after pictures to show reconnecting a loop and claiming green, plus paired examples of dangerous self-crossing and safe backtracking to undo the latest part of a line. The Freeze hint reads “Earn a Freeze every 45% claimed.” The charge text stays bright even while unavailable; a ready Freeze has an icy glow, moving shimmer, and snowflake. Reduced-motion mode keeps the ready styling static. Ghost behaviors and red surges are left for players to discover.

For testing, press **↑ ↑ ↓ ↓ → ← → B A** followed immediately by **two digits** for the wave, with no other keyboard presses in between. For example, **↑ ↑ ↓ ↓ → ← → B A 0 4** starts wave 4 and **↑ ↑ ↓ ↓ → ← → B A 2 0** starts wave 20. Waves 01–99 are accepted; 00 is ignored. Number-row and numpad digits both work. The code waits for both digits before starting the selected wave, from the menu or during a game, with three lives and zero Freeze charges. The score is locked to zero for that testing run, including capture rewards and wave bonuses; the saved best score is untouched. The lock continues into later waves. Choose New game for a normal scoring run. Auto-repeat does not count as another key press; modifiers or unrelated keys interrupt the sequence.

For infinite lives, press **↑ ↑ ↓ ↓ ← → ← → B A** with no intervening key presses. This enables testing on the current board (or starts wave 1 from the menu), locks score at zero, and displays ∞ for lives. Hits still clear the unfinished line and respawn the player, but no lives are spent and the run cannot end from collisions. It carries through later waves and can be combined with the wave-selection code in either order. Both testing modes leave the saved best untouched and reset with New game.

P, Escape, or Pause pauses the simulation; leaving the page pauses automatically. The pause screen offers New game. Sound starts off and can be enabled at any time. Reduced-motion preferences disable decorative flashes, particles, and ghost bobbing. Start with three lives. Claimed cells earn 10 points each; completing a wave adds `500 × wave + 100 × remaining lives`. Next neighborhood carries score and lives forward, resets the board and Freeze charges, and adds one ghost. There is no time limit. Your best score is saved under `eviction.best.v1`; unavailable storage falls back to the current session.

## Dad Dug

Open `dad-dug.html`, or choose Dad Dug from the arcade. Dig tunnels, face Inky or Blinky, and hold the pump for four inflation hits. Both ghosts flash blue and white and grow while inflated; they stop moving and gradually deflate if you stop pumping. The hose reaches three tiles through open tunnels and cannot pass through dirt or rocks. Its tip extends at 120 board units per second, taking about 0.87 seconds to reach full range. Only the advancing tip can attach to a ghost; the hose behind it cannot. Once attached, holding Pump adds inflation every 0.27 seconds. Misses retract over 0.16 seconds, cannot catch ghosts on the return trip, and retry after a short recovery while Pump stays held. Releasing Pump or turning cancels the current extension. Ghosts follow tunnels and periodically phase through dirt, becoming unpumpable until they return to a tunnel. Ghost movement is 20% faster than the original pace, including phasing and later-round speed increases. Inky warns with an exclamation mark before breathing icy fire down a tunnel.

Move with arrows or WASD; hold Space or X to pump in the direction you face. Hold a direction while pumping to turn in place. On touchscreens, hold the on-screen D-pad and Pump buttons, including together. A standard gamepad uses the left stick or D-pad to move and the south face button or right trigger to pump. South selects on the start, pause, and game-over screens; Start/Options toggles pause. Release controller buttons and sticks after changing screens or returning to the page. Keyboard P/Escape or the Pause button also pauses; leaving the page pauses automatically. New game is available while paused. Sound is optional and starts off.

Dig underneath a rock to release it after a short wobble, then step aside. Rock kills earn 1,000 points for the first ghost and 2,000 for each additional ghost under the same rock. Pump kills earn 200–500 points according to depth. The second rock drop reveals a carrot worth 500 points for 12 seconds. Clear every ghost to earn a round bonus and advance; later rounds add ghosts and increase their speed. Start with three lives and earn another every 10,000 points. A lost life keeps the excavated tunnels and surviving ghosts. The local best score uses `dadDug.best.v1`; storage restrictions leave the game playable with a session-only best.

## Ghost Pop

Open `ghost-pop.html` in a browser, or choose Ghost Pop from the arcade. No build is required; keep the shared arcade assets beside it. The Other Games link returns to the project root.

Classic uses 6 colors, 18 columns, 20 rows, and 7 starting rows. Every 5 non-popping shots adds 2 rows. Popping ghosts preserves that counter. At the last chance, the next-color preview becomes a row warning; eliminated colors cannot return through the queue or row waves.

Easy inherits Classic with 5 colors and 7 non-popping shots per two-row wave. Super-Easy inherits Classic with 4 colors and 7 non-popping shots per one-row wave. Each built-in mode has its own local top 10 scores.

Drag and release to shoot with touch or the left mouse button. Right/middle clicks do not shoot. Arrow keys aim and Space/Enter fires during play; form controls keep their usual keyboard behavior. Arrow presses turn 0.025 radians; holding repeats after 100 ms, turning 0.015 radians every 25 ms independently of the operating system's repeat settings. `AIM_INPUT` controls these values. Classic has a 340-unit aim guide with one previewed wall bounce and slightly forgiving collision detection. Actual shots have unlimited wall bounces. The Custom Mode bounce setting applies only to the guide. The next shot is shown immediately to the right of the launcher, including the last-chance row warning.

Gamepads use the browser's [standard gamepad mapping](https://w3c.github.io/gamepad/#remapping). Connect a controller and press a button so the browser can detect it. Either stick points the aim into the upper firing arc; downward stick directions are mirrored upward. A radial deadzone prevents drift. Either trigger or the south face button (Xbox A, Switch B, PlayStation X) shoots once per press, without automatic firing when held through a landing or pop animation. `GAMEPAD_INPUT` controls the deadzone, trigger threshold, and navigation repeat timing.

The D-pad moves between menu buttons and settings controls; south selects, east goes back, and Start/Options opens or closes settings during a game. Left/right adjusts the focused speed selector. Custom numeric settings and high-score names still use keyboard/touch input. Controller presses are suppressed while the page is unfocused; release held buttons after returning. Mouse, touch, and keyboard remain available when the Gamepad API is unavailable.

Matches pop as a chain reaction along same-color connections, starting with the landed shot and advancing one adjacency layer every 85 ms (`REACTION_INTERVAL`). Detached ghosts disappear after the matching waves finish; points, queue changes, and win/loss checks resolve once at the end. Another shot cannot launch during a chain. Restarting or returning to the menu cancels the pending animation.

The pop-count prefix appears only when total removed ghosts exceed the active `matchSize`. Matched-ghost chain bonuses begin only for matches larger than that configured minimum: `1 + max(0, matched - matchSize) * chainBonus`. Falling ghosts retain their separate `dropPoints` reward.

Final scores use `points / shots^1.5` on a loss and `(points * 100) / shots^1.5` on a win. Fewer shots improve the final score for the same points and result. The top 10 completed games for each built-in mode are saved on this browser. Qualifying results include an inline name field; unnamed scores display Anonymous. Custom Mode saves preferences and does not record scores.

Settings are available from the menu and during play. Colorblind mode is off by default; enabling it adds large, centered color numbers to ghosts, shots, and the next-shot preview. Color assist is also off by default. When enabled, current-shot matches pulse dark blue for 0.7 seconds and white for 0.3 seconds, next-shot matches are cyan (except at the last chance), and other ghosts are orange. The current color takes priority when current and next match. Assist tracks the projectile color during flight.

Game speed defaults to Fast: shots travel at 845 board units/second (30% faster than the original 650), with the original mouth-animation rate. Slow travels at 455 units/second (30% slower), with half-speed mouth animation. The assist pulse always uses real time. Opening Settings pauses the game until the dialog closes.

## Changing rules

The script's `CLASSIC` object is the default rule set; `MODES` supplies each mode's name, rules, and leaderboard policy. Starting a game copies the selected rules into `rules`, and geometry, matching, scoring, aiming, collisions, and row waves read those values. `FIELDS` defines Custom Mode controls and validation bounds. Custom defaults come directly from Classic. Custom supports 1–36 ghost colors, 4–48 columns, 6–60 rows, up to 100 preview bounces, and a guide up to 10,000 units. Starting rows must leave two empty rows and a row wave must be smaller than the grid.

Every Randomize colors button samples each color independently from the full RGB range, without hue-spacing constraints. Close or identical colors are permitted and persist as chosen. Default colors restores a preset palette, including green `#19864b` in Classic. Display settings do not change the underlying color IDs or matching rules.

LocalStorage keys are `pacGhostPop.custom.v1`, `pacGhostPop.palettes.v1`, `pacGhostPop.scores.v1`, and `pacGhostPop.settings.v1`. They persist across browser restarts under the same origin/browser profile. Clearing browser data removes them. Browser restrictions on local files/private sessions may prevent persistence; the game displays a notice and remains playable. Keep the file at the same path when playing directly from disk.
